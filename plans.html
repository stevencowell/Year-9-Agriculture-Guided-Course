Plans and diagrams

The supplied cattle-yard material contains animal-handling diagrams and a facility checklist. It is not a construction plan.

Plan-reading theory

How to read the supplied cattle-handling diagrams

Begin with the title and purpose. A handling diagram explains a relationship or movement principle; it does not automatically state construction dimensions. Identify the animal, handler position, direction arrows, barriers and named features. Then describe only what the symbols support.

Flight-zone and point-of-balance diagrams help explain why handler position can influence cattle movement. The yard checklist directs attention to features such as gates, fencing, the crush and the loading ramp. These sources support a safety analysis, not an assumed school-yard layout.

Plan-reading checkpoints

  • State whether the source is a plan, diagram, map, checklist or photograph.
  • Use the legend, labels and arrows before interpreting movement.
  • Do not infer scale, dimensions, quantities, capacity or construction details that are not shown.
  • Connect a visible feature to a safety purpose, then refer local action to the teacher.
Drawing boundary: this source does not authorise a cattle-yard construction plan, school-farm site plan, dimensions, capacity or layout.

Use the supplied handling diagrams
